# A Short Note on Diffusion Limits

Diffusion processes have a characteristic timescale that scales with the
square of the relevant length. This is why mixing a cup of coffee takes
seconds but mixing a swimming pool by diffusion alone would take years.
The squared dependence is what makes stirring useful: stirring introduces
convective transport whose timescale is linear in length, breaking the
diffusion bottleneck for any non-trivial volume.

The same principle explains why cells are small. A cell relying on
diffusion to deliver nutrients from its surface to its centre is bound by
this squared scaling — double the radius, quadruple the delivery time.
Eukaryotes evolved around this constraint with active intracellular
transport.
